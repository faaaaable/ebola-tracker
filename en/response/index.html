The response and its challenges

Situation as of 5 Sep 2026

What is being done against the outbreak? What are the main difficulties? Two questions, two parts, from the INSP bulletins.

Part one

The response

01

Alerts

what surveillance receives, and what it keeps

An alert is a report — a sick person, a death, a rumour — that teams must go and check. Roughly one in five is validated as a suspected case and sampled. Many alerts means surveillance is looking; the validated share says what it finds.

02

Laboratory

samples tested and positive results

Positivity is the share of samples that confirm the virus. It measures how well tests are targeted, not how many people are infected: when it rises while the number of samples stalls, only the obvious patients are being sampled and others are being missed.

03

Contact follow-up

exposed people, seen or lost from view

Every confirmed case leaves contacts to follow for twenty-one days. Their number is the size of the outbreak as the response sees it; the share seen each day is the grip it keeps on it — below the threshold, exposed people transmit undetected.

04

Treatment centres

patients hospitalised and beds available

A saturated treatment centre pushes patients back into the community, where deaths occur outside care. Occupancy relates hospitalised patients — confirmed and suspected — to the beds the bulletin declares; it exceeds 100% where patients are placed outside the planned beds.

05

Place of death

in the community or in a treatment centre

Two confirmed deaths in three occur outside treatment centres. That share says what the response does not reach: a patient who dies at home was neither isolated nor treated, and exposed their family to the end.

Weekly share of confirmed deaths that occurred in the community rather than in a treatment centre, nationwide. The dotted line marks the average over the weeks covered: that is the result, not the dips and bumps. Bulletins only distinguish the two places since 13 July.

Method and sources

Sources: INSP situation reports, read bulletin by bulletin. The form of these figures has changed with each editorial period; a bulletin whose wording could not be read leaves an empty day rather than a guessed value. Alerts and laboratory figures are aggregated by week, Monday to Sunday, the current week appearing only once complete. No figure is recalculated in place of the source, except occupancy when the bulletin gives patients and beds without the rate.

  1. 01

    Patients who refuse isolation, families who refuse decontamination

    The most constant obstacle: it appears in almost every bulletin since the first one that counted its difficulties, on 31 May. The form changes, the substance does not. Reluctance to swabbing in June; a positive body taken away to the family cemetery of Misebere, in Katwa zone, on 10 August; a burial refused “for religious convenience” in Bunia and three isolated people escaping overnight in Poko on 18 August. On 3 September, Ituri still records 21 refusals of isolation in a single day, and a Kalunguta team is assaulted in the Mataba area.

    Cited in 90 bulletins, from SitRep 017 (31 May 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). IturiNord-KivuTshopo

  2. 02

    Risky burials and bodies not swabbed

    A body that is not swabbed is a death that enters no count, and a burial without precautions is a chain of transmission. The bulletins say so from 31 May, with “reluctance to post-mortem swabbing”. On 24 July an attack at the Muchanga bridge paralyses burial teams in Nyankunde. On 10 August, ten failures in Mangala and four in Komanda. On 3 September, nine bodies go unswabbed in Bambu, Fataki and Mandima, “due to resistance”.

    Cited in 90 bulletins, from SitRep 017 (31 May 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). IturiNord-KivuHaut-Uélé

  3. 03

    Saturated treatment centres

    From 31 May, capacity is judged insufficient “despite the opening of Rwampara”. Ituri saturates at 103% on 18 June, North Kivu at 160% on 9 July; on 15 July the Bambu centre shows 316% occupancy. Extensions announced for Bambu and Nizi are still awaited in mid-August. On 3 September, North Kivu remains above 100% and more than half of its patients are treated in non-standard facilities. The treatment-centre curve above shows the same thing in figures.

    Cited in 88 bulletins, from SitRep 017 (31 May 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). Nord-KivuIturiHaut-Uélé

  4. 04

    Contacts lost to follow-up

    On 31 May, 43% of contacts are seen, against a 95% target. On 19 June, 69%. On 17 July the bulletin speaks of “sharp deterioration”: 61% in Ituri, 54% in Haut-Uélé. Since August the displayed target has come down to 85%, and the national rate exceeds it; but Bas-Uélé stays under 70%, “linked to insufficient dedicated teams”, the 3 September bulletin still says. A contact not seen is an exposed person who can fall ill without being expected.

    Cited in 90 bulletins, from SitRep 017 (31 May 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). IturiHaut-UéléBas-Uélé

  5. 05

    What is missing: drugs, supplies, blood, food

    The first bulletin to count its difficulties notes a “supply failure in essential medicines”. In late June, samples wait at the North Kivu laboratory. On 22 July, protective equipment and chlorine are short, and 175 triage units work out of 1,170. In mid-August, dozens of patients hospitalised in Ituri have “no food support”. On 3 September, Tshopo has had no safe blood for six days and its technical platform allows neither biochemistry nor haematology.

    Cited in 87 bulletins, from SitRep 017 (31 May 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). IturiNord-KivuTshopo

  6. 06

    Insecurity, which creates access problems

    An ADF incursion is reported as early as 2 June. “Insecurity and limited access (CODECO, ADF)” recurs verbatim from July to early August. The Muchanga bridge attack on 24 July stops the teams in Nyankunde. In mid-August, bulletins explain incomplete decontaminations by “insecurity in some villages” and untraceable addresses. On 3 September, the Avakubi control point remains closed “for security reasons”. Where teams cannot enter, the virus is neither counted nor followed.

    Cited in 62 bulletins, from SitRep 019 (2 Jun 2026) to SitRep 113 (4 Sep 2026). IturiNord-KivuHaut-Uélé

  7. 07

    Workers paid late or not paid

    Payment enters the difficulties on 12 July. On the 26th, the Bunia and Rwampara zones are on strike, and work resumes only after the military governor steps in. On 10 August, North Kivu's psychosocial pillar announces its own. On 15 August the strike deprives six Haut-Uélé control points of any reporting; on the 19th, five workers out of eighteen are paid in North Kivu. On 3 September, four Ituri control points are “on strike for lack of payment”. Forty-six bulletins mention it.

    Cited in 47 bulletins, from SitRep 059 (12 Jul 2026) to SitRep 112 (3 Sep 2026). IturiHaut-UéléNord-Kivu

  8. 08

    Control points that do not control

    Entry and control points are meant to spot travelling patients. On 3 August, 9 of Haut-Uélé's 38 priority points are active, and 4 of Tshopo's 19, just as two new zones are reached along the Nia-Nia axis. On 14 August, 26% are activated in Haut-Uélé, “for lack of training, supervision and support”. Since late August, only one of Ituri's eleven strategic sites works without interruption, and Magambe's counts 209 refusals of screening in a single day.

    Cited in 52 bulletins, from SitRep 060 (13 Jul 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). Haut-UéléNord-KivuIturi

  9. 09

    Teams and health workers attacked

    The bulletins never count them, but they have reported them since 17 July: “insecurity targeting burial teams”, an attack at the Muchanga bridge, work paralysed in Nyankunde, the same sentence nineteen bulletins in a row. Then the facts sharpen. Workers and police assaulted at the Mukulia control point on 10 and 11 August. On the 19th, a team escorted by the army in Mungamba and the homes of two community relays targeted by arson. On the 20th, two Red Cross vehicles vandalised near Isiro, an ambulance attacked in Pawa. On the 24th, a burial team member killed in a road accident in Bambu, two others assaulted in Rwampara. On the 26th, the murder of a community animation cell chairman in North Kivu. On 4 September, residents threaten to burn the treatment centre under construction in Katwa.

    Cited in 33 bulletins, from SitRep 064 (17 Jul 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). IturiNord-KivuTshopo

  10. 10

    Complex reporting of information

    What the response does not measure, it cannot correct, and the site cannot show. On 17 July, North Kivu does not report its beds, and the occupancy rate is computed on the previous day's. On 7 August, “reporting completeness” is named as an obstacle to decision-making. On 26 August it stands at 83% nationally, 55% in Haut-Uélé and South Kivu. On 3 September, South Kivu “did not report” its alerts at all. On this site those days stay empty: when the source does not say, the site does not say.

    Cited in 38 bulletins, from SitRep 064 (17 Jul 2026) to SitRep 114 (5 Sep 2026). IturiHaut-UéléTshopo
