Ebola in Kenya

Situation as of 17 October 2026

On 6 October 2026 Kenya confirmed its first case of Bundibugyo Ebola, a patient who had come from the DRC and died in Nairobi. From 14 to 17 October six more cases were confirmed in relatives and health workers of the first patient, all already in quarantine. The full account

Kenya confirmed its first case on 6 October 2026. The patient, a Kenyan who lived in the DRC, had been treated there in several health facilities. On 2 October he travelled by road to Kampala via Beni, then flew to Nairobi, arriving on 3 October.

Isolated at Nairobi Hospital, he died on the night of 5 October and was buried on 6 October following the safe burial protocol. Bundibugyo virus was detected by the National Virology Reference Laboratory and by the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI).

In the first days, 28 contacts, including relatives and health workers, were being followed, as were the 23 passengers and 4 crew members of the flight. As of 13 October 2026, the Kenyan Ministry of Health listed 128 contacts of the first patient, 113 of whom had been traced: 108 in quarantine in Kenya, checked daily for 21 days, and 5 outside the country, reported to the authorities there. As of 17 October, it counts 130 contacts listed, 128 of them (98.5%) traced and in quarantine; the remaining 2 are still being traced.

On 14 October 2026, the ministry confirmed a second case: a 39-year-old man, a close relative of the first patient, already in quarantine and checked every day. He fell ill on 13 October (fever, fatigue, joint pain) and was isolated at once; he tested positive at the National Virology Reference Laboratory and at the KEMRI-CDC laboratory, and is being cared for in a designated treatment centre. According to the ministry, the two cases are linked and there is no evidence of spread in the wider community.

On 16 October 2026, the ministry confirmed a third case: a 65-year-old woman, a close relative and primary contact of the first two patients, in quarantine since 6 October. As soon as she developed fever, sore throat and malaise she was isolated and tested; she tested positive at the National Virology Reference Laboratory and the KEMRI laboratory, and was transferred to the Infectious Disease Unit, where she is receiving supportive care. The second patient is in stable condition. The ministry considers the risk to the public low, as there is no community transmission, and warns that further cases among quarantined contacts remain possible.

On 17 October 2026, the ministry confirmed four new cases, bringing the total to seven, including the first patient, who died. They come from two quarantine sites: a close male relative of the first patient, in quarantine since 6 October, and three health workers, two men and a woman aged 29, 35 and 32, who had cared for him in hospital on 3 and 4 October and had been in quarantine since the 9th. All first developed fever, were isolated at once and tested positive at the National Virology Reference Laboratory and the KEMRI laboratory; they are receiving supportive care at the designated treatment facility. The second and third patients are stable. The ministry is closely monitoring four family members of the first patient and all the health workers who cared for him.

The country had been preparing since May: more than 652,000 travellers screened on entry, 267 suspected samples tested and about 5,000 health workers trained. By 15 October, 685,027 travellers had been screened; those arriving from the 10 high-risk countries fill in a digital form before boarding. Kenya imposes no restrictions on travel, trade or public gatherings.

Frequently asked questions

Is there Ebola in Kenya?

Yes. Kenya confirmed its first Ebola case on 6 October 2026, a patient who had come from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and died in Nairobi. On 14 October 2026 a second case was confirmed: a close relative of the first patient, who was already in quarantine. On 16 October, a third: a close relative of the first two, also in quarantine. On 17 October, four more: a close relative and three health workers who had cared for the first patient, all already in quarantine. The country has 7 confirmed cases and 1 death; according to the ministry, the cases are linked and there is no evidence of community transmission.

Who was the first patient and how did he reach Kenya?

A Kenyan who lived in the DRC, where he had been treated in several health facilities. On 2 October he travelled by road to Kampala, Uganda, via Beni, then flew to Nairobi, arriving on 3 October. Isolated at Nairobi Hospital, he died there on the night of 5 October.

Who is the second patient?

A 39-year-old man, a close relative of the first patient. He was in quarantine and checked every day when he fell ill on 13 October 2026. Isolated at the first symptoms, he was confirmed on 14 October and is receiving care in a designated treatment centre. On 16 and 17 October the ministry said he was in stable condition.

Who is the third patient?

A 65-year-old woman, a close relative and primary contact of the first two patients. In quarantine since 6 October 2026, she was isolated as soon as she developed fever, sore throat and malaise. Confirmed on 16 October, she is receiving supportive care in the Infectious Disease Unit. On 17 October the ministry said she was in stable condition.

Who are the four patients confirmed on 17 October?

A close male relative of the first patient, in quarantine since 6 October 2026, and three health workers (two men and a woman aged 29, 35 and 32) who had cared for the first patient in hospital on 3 and 4 October, in quarantine since the 9th. All first developed fever and were isolated at once; they are receiving supportive care at the designated treatment facility.

How many people are being followed in Kenya?

As of 13 October 2026, 128 contacts of the first patient had been listed and 113 traced: 108 are in quarantine in Kenya, checked daily for 21 days, and 5 are outside the country. The remaining 15 are still being traced. As of 17 October, the ministry counts 130 contacts listed, 128 of them (98.5%) traced and in quarantine, above the 85% global benchmark; the remaining 2 are being traced. It is closely monitoring four family members of the first patient and all the health workers who cared for him.

Which Ebola virus is it?

Bundibugyo virus, the one behind the ongoing DRC outbreak. For the first patient it was detected by the National Virology Reference Laboratory and by the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI); for the second, by the National Virology Reference Laboratory and the KEMRI-CDC laboratory; for the third and the four cases of 17 October, by the National Virology Reference Laboratory and the KEMRI laboratory.

Was Kenya prepared?

Yes, since May 2026: more than 652,000 travellers were screened on entering the country, 267 suspected samples were tested and about 5,000 health workers were trained.
